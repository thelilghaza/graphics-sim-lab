Important Engineering Observations
Empirically Grounded Findings across Project 03
  • M1 Harness Rigor: Monotonic nanosecond-resolution timing with explicit compiler barriers (DoNotOptimize and clobber_memory) successfully isolates CPU execution duration from optimization dead-code elimination.
  • M2 Cache Locality: Structure of Arrays (SoA) layout achieved 1.67x higher measured throughput than Array of Structures (AoS) for field-heavy sequential traversals by eliminating unread struct attributes from loaded 64-byte cache lines.
  • M3 Vectorization Boundaries: AVX2 intrinsic acceleration provided 3.84x measured speedup over scalar loops for compute-heavy vector dot products, while memory-bandwidth-bound AXPY kernels exhibited modest 1.40x improvement once bus saturation was reached.
  • M4 Contention Scaling: The Vyukov sequence-number MPMC non-blocking atomic queue provided 3.79x higher throughput than the Mutex baseline under uncontended 1P/1C conditions. Under oversubscribed worker contention (8P/8C), Mutex OS thread blocking mitigated cache-line bus invalidations, narrowing the throughput difference.
  • M5 Lifetime Economics: Monotonic linear arena bulk reset demonstrated 20.08x lower total execution time than per-item general-purpose malloc/free in frame-bounded temporary allocation patterns, illustrating the benefit of bulk lifetime management over individual object deallocation.
Methodology & Data Provenance
Benchmarking Protocol Rules
  • Monotonic Clock Source: C++20 std::chrono::steady_clock recording microsecond iteration timings.
  • Warmup & Iteration Model: 5 un-timed warmup runs followed by 100 timed iterations (2 warmups / 10 iterations for long workloads).
  • Statistical Reporting: Mean, median, standard deviation, min, max, ops/sec, and MB/s ($1 ext{ MB} = 10^6 ext{ bytes}$).
  • Timing Isolation: Worker thread creation, preallocated bookkeeping arrays, and validation verification occur strictly outside the timed execution window.
